To send money from Cameroon to Nigeria in 2026, you have four realistic routes: a stablecoin transfer through USDT, a money transfer operator like Western Union or Ria, a bank wire, or a physical cash changer at the border. For most people moving between 100,000 and 5,000,000 FCFA, the USDT route is the fastest and the cheapest, usually landing in a Nigerian bank account the same day. The bank wire is the slowest and the most expensive. The border changer is the riskiest.

The reason is simple: there is no direct, cheap banking corridor between the CFA franc zone and the naira. Cameroon and Nigeria share a long border and a huge amount of trade, but almost nothing links XAF and NGN at the banking level, so every route converts through the dollar somewhere. The question is not whether you pay a conversion spread. It is how many spreads you pay, and who takes them. Below: the four routes compared with real costs, what the naira to FCFA rate actually is this week, and the three mistakes our desk cleans up almost every month.

How does the naira to FCFA route through USDT actually work?

It is three steps, and it looks more complicated on paper than it is in practice.

Step one: your FCFA becomes USDT. You send MoMo or Orange Money to a desk, and USDT arrives in your wallet. On our side we quote you a single FCFA per USDT number that already includes our margin, so there is no second fee waiting later. Our page on buying and selling USDT in Cameroon covers the mechanics and the limits.

Step two: the USDT moves to Nigeria. This is the part that costs almost nothing. A transfer on the Tron network runs about a dollar regardless of whether you are sending 200 USDT or 20,000. This is exactly why the route beats banks: the international leg, the expensive part everywhere else, is nearly free here. Use TRC20 and confirm the network in writing, because sending on the wrong chain is the one genuinely expensive mistake, as we explain in USDT TRC20 versus ERC20.

Step three: the USDT becomes naira. Your recipient in Nigeria sells the USDT to a Nigerian desk or a verified P2P counterparty, and naira lands in their bank account, usually within minutes because Nigerian bank transfers are instant.

The whole thing typically settles in under an hour when both sides are awake and ready. If you are sending recurring amounts, for school fees or supplier stock, ask your recipient to keep a Nigerian bank account and a wallet ready before you start rather than setting them up mid-transfer. What is the naira to FCFA rate today?

As of 4 August 2026, the official market rate sits near 1 XAF to 2.39 NGN, which is roughly 1,000 FCFA for 2,390 naira, or 100,000 naira for about 41,800 FCFA. You can check the current level on any neutral source such as the Central Bank of Nigeria reference page before you accept a quote.

That number is a reference, not a price you will get. Every route quotes you something below it, and the difference is the spread. Three things move it:

The dollar leg. The CFA franc is pegged to the euro, so XAF to NGN is really XAF to EUR to USD to NGN. The naira side is the volatile one. When the naira moves in a week, your FCFA cost moves with it even though nothing changed in Cameroon.

Direction of demand. More Cameroonians sending to Nigeria than the reverse means naira is in demand and the rate tightens against you. In the weeks before Nigerian school terms and after big Aba and Onitsha buying trips, we see this clearly.

Time of day and week. Weekend and late night pricing is worse everywhere, because desks hold less inventory when they cannot rebalance. We keep our current pricing on the rates page, and our dedicated naira to FCFA page explains how we set it.

How much does it really cost to send 500,000 FCFA to Nigeria?

Take a concrete case, since percentages hide things. You want to send 500,000 FCFA to a supplier in Lagos.

The MoMo leg comes first and people forget it. Getting 500,000 FCFA out of mobile money is not free. Orange Money publishes a grid where withdrawals in the 5,000 to 399,999 FCFA band cost 1% of the amount plus 4 FCFA, and the 400,000 to 500,000 band is a flat 4,004 FCFA, on top of the 0.2% state transfer levy that applies across the sector. You can confirm current numbers on the official Orange Money tariff page, and MTN publishes its own grid. If you are sending directly from your MoMo balance to a desk rather than withdrawing cash first, you skip the withdrawal fee entirely, which is worth several thousand FCFA on this size.

Then the conversion spread. This is the real cost. On the USDT route at typical desk pricing, expect somewhere between 1% and 3% all in across both conversions, so roughly 5,000 to 15,000 FCFA on this transfer. Through a money transfer operator, the same 500,000 FCFA commonly loses 4% to 8% once you compare the naira delivered against the market rate, so 20,000 to 40,000 FCFA.

Then the network fee, about 600 FCFA on Tron, and it does not grow with the amount. On a 5,000,000 FCFA transfer it is still about 600 FCFA, which is why the advantage of this route widens sharply as the amount grows.

The honest summary: below about 50,000 FCFA, the differences are small enough that convenience wins and any route is fine. Above 200,000 FCFA, the spread is the whole game and it is worth ten minutes of comparison.

What goes wrong when sending money from Cameroon to Nigeria?

Three things, and we see all three regularly.

The recipient is not ready. Someone sends USDT to a cousin in Nigeria who has no wallet, no verified P2P account and no idea what to do. The funds sit for days. Fix this before you send, not after: confirm your recipient has a working wallet, knows which network to expect, and has a Nigerian bank account in their own name.

The "trader" on the Nigerian side is not real. Cross border transfers attract impostors because both parties are strangers to each other and each assumes the other side did the vetting. Nobody did. A fake counterparty takes the USDT and disappears, and there is no reversal on any blockchain. The patterns are the same ones we documented in WhatsApp crypto scams in Cameroon, and the checks in our safety guide apply identically across the border.

The MoMo leg gets flagged. Large or unusual mobile money activity can trigger a hold on your account, especially if you suddenly move amounts far outside your normal pattern. Keep your transfers consistent with how you normally use the account, keep records of what each transfer was for, and read MoMo scams and account safety before moving large sums. If you also need to cash out on the Cameroon side, our guide to selling bitcoin for MoMo covers the same discipline in reverse.

One habit prevents most of it. Send a small test transfer first, always. Ten dollars, wait for confirmation that it landed and was converted, then send the rest. It costs a dollar in network fees and it has saved our clients far more than that.

FAQ

What is the cheapest way to send money from Cameroon to Nigeria?
For amounts above roughly 200,000 FCFA, converting through USDT is normally cheapest, because the international leg costs about a dollar no matter how much you send and you only pay conversion spreads on each end. Money transfer operators and banks bundle a much wider spread into the rate they quote, which typically costs 4% to 10% once you compare the naira actually delivered. Below about 50,000 FCFA the difference is small and convenience matters more.
How long does a transfer from Cameroon to Nigeria take?
Through USDT, usually under an hour when both parties are ready, because Nigerian bank transfers settle instantly once the crypto side is done. Money transfer operators are fast for the transfer itself but depend on the payout branch having cash available. Bank wires from Cameroon to Nigeria commonly take three to seven working days and can take longer if a compliance check is triggered.
Is it legal to send money from Cameroon to Nigeria using crypto?
Holding and transferring crypto is not criminalized for private individuals in Cameroon, though the CFA franc is the only legal tender and regional banking rules restrict financial institutions from handling crypto transactions directly. That means these transfers happen outside the formal banking system. Anyone doing this commercially rather than personally has registration and tax obligations, so if you are moving business volumes, take proper advice rather than assuming.
What naira rate should I expect for my FCFA?
Expect a rate a little below the market reference, because every route takes a spread. As of August 2026 the market reference is near 2.39 naira per FCFA. A quote within a few percent of that is normal and fair. A quote 8% or more below it means the operator has hidden a large fee inside the rate, which is the most common way people overpay without noticing.
Can I send money to Nigeria straight from MTN MoMo or Orange Money?
Not directly to a Nigerian bank account, no, because there is no live retail corridor between the two systems. What you can do is send your MoMo or Orange Money balance to a desk in Cameroon, receive USDT, and have that converted to naira on the Nigerian side. Sending from your MoMo balance rather than withdrawing cash first also saves you the withdrawal fee, which is several thousand FCFA on larger amounts.
