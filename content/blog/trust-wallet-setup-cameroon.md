You install the official app, create a new wallet, write your twelve recovery words on paper, confirm them, and you have an address that can receive bitcoin or USDT from anywhere in the world. First, install the real app and nothing else, because fake wallet clones exist specifically to capture the twelve words you type into them. Second, write those words on paper and never type them into any website, form, support chat or "verification" page for the rest of your life. Everything else in this guide is detail. ## What is Trust Wallet and why do Cameroonians use it?

Trust Wallet is a mobile crypto wallet, meaning an app that stores your private keys on your own phone. That is different from an exchange account, where the platform holds the coins and you hold a login. With a wallet, you are the bank. Nobody can suspend your account, ask you for a document, or hold your funds during a review.

For Cameroon that matters for practical reasons more than ideological ones. A trader in Douala who receives USDT from a client in Dubai needs somewhere to receive it that does not require passing a foreign platform's verification. A student paying part of a tuition balance needs an address that works on a Sunday.

Trust Wallet supports well over a hundred networks, including Bitcoin, Ethereum, Tron and BNB Chain, which covers everything a Cameroonian user meets in normal trading. It works on a modest Android phone and on any connection that can load WhatsApp. That is the whole appeal: it is boring, widely supported, and free.

The trade off is real and you should understand it before you start. Self custody means no password reset. If you lose your phone and your twelve words, the money is gone permanently, and there is no support desk on earth that can recover it. That is not a flaw in the app, it is how the technology works.

## How do you set up Trust Wallet in Cameroon step by step?

This is the sequence we run with clients on a call, in order, and the order matters.

1. **Install from the official store only.** Open Google Play or the App Store and search Trust Wallet, or go straight to [trustwallet.com](https://trustwallet.com/) and use its download link. Check the developer name and the install count before you tap install. Never install a wallet from an APK link someone sent you, and never from a link inside a Telegram group.
2. **Choose "Create a new wallet".** The other option imports an existing wallet, which you only use if you already have twelve words of your own. If anyone ever tells you to import their words into your phone so you can "withdraw a balance", that is a known scam and the money will vanish the second it lands.
3. **Set a passcode and turn on biometrics.** This locks the app on your device. It is not your backup, it only stops someone who picks up your unlocked phone.
4. **Write down the twelve words on paper, in order, by hand.** Not a screenshot. Not a note in your phone. Not a WhatsApp message to yourself. Paper, in your own handwriting, numbered one to twelve. Make two copies and keep them in two different physical places.
5. **Confirm the words in the app.** Trust Wallet asks you to tap them back in the correct order. This step exists to prove you actually wrote them down. Do not rush it.
6. **Send a small test first.** Before you receive any real amount, have someone send you 5 dollars of USDT and watch it arrive. Confirming the address works while nothing is at stake is worth five minutes.

That is the entire setup. Notice that nothing asked for your name, your ID or your phone number. If an app or a person asks for those "to activate the wallet", you are not using Trust Wallet, you are being farmed.

## How do you receive USDT in your Trust Wallet?

Receiving is where beginners actually lose money, so read this part twice.

Open the app, tap Receive, then search for the coin. Here is the catch that catches everyone: USDT is not one thing. There is USDT on Tron, USDT on Ethereum, and USDT on BNB Chain. Each one has a different address format and they are not interchangeable. In Trust Wallet you must select the exact version, for example "Tether (Tron)", and copy the address that belongs to that network.

Then send that address to whoever is paying you, as plain text, in the chat. Never send it as a screenshot, and never let anyone send you an address as a screenshot, because you cannot copy from an image and typing an address by hand is how people lose funds to one wrong character.

Three habits that have saved our clients real money:

- **Read four characters back.** After you paste an address anywhere, check the first four and last four characters against the original. Clipboard hijacking malware quietly swaps the address between your copy and your paste. This is a documented attack, not a theory.
- **Agree the network in writing before the payment.** Ask the sender to confirm "TRC20" or "ERC20" in the chat as its own question, separate from the address.
- **Test with a small amount on any new address.** Every single time. Ten dollars, confirm arrival, then send the rest.

## Which network should you use for USDT in Cameroon?

Use Tron, in almost every case. Fees change with network conditions, so confirm before you send, but the shape of the difference is consistent.

| Network | Ticker you will see | Typical transfer cost | Speed | Use it when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tron | TRC20 | Roughly a dollar | 1 to 3 minutes | Default for USDT, suppliers, remittances, everyday amounts |
| BNB Chain | BEP20 | Cents | Under a minute | The sender insists, or you are moving funds inside Binance's ecosystem |
| Ethereum | ERC20 | Several dollars, sometimes far more | 2 to 10 minutes | Only when the sender genuinely cannot use anything else |
| Bitcoin network | BTC | Varies with congestion | 10 to 60 minutes | Bitcoin itself, never USDT |

The mistake we clean up most often is USDT sent on the wrong chain: the sender used ERC20, the receiver gave a TRC20 address, and the funds are now sitting somewhere the receiver's app does not display. Sometimes that is recoverable if the wallet supports the other network, often it is slow, and occasionally it is permanent. One question in the chat prevents all of it.

## Is Trust Wallet safe, and how do people actually lose money?

The app itself is not usually the problem. Almost every loss we hear about on the desk falls into one of four patterns, and all four are avoidable.

**The fake app.** Cloned wallets that look identical to the real one, distributed through phishing links and social posts, sometimes slipping briefly onto app stores before removal. Researchers documented a batch of 26 such apps mimicking Trust Wallet, MetaMask, Ledger and others in April 2026, reported by [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/04/26-fakewallet-apps-found-on-apple-app.html). Their only job is to capture the seed phrase you type in. Install from the official store, check the developer, and never import your words into an app you were told about in a group chat.

**The support impostor.** You post a problem publicly, a friendly account replies within minutes offering help, and eventually asks you to "verify your wallet" on a form or to share your twelve words. Trust Wallet support will never ask for your recovery phrase. No legitimate service ever will, including us.

**The screenshot backup.** Someone photographs their twelve words, that photo syncs to Google Photos, then to a laptop, and ends up in a cloud account whose password was reused on a site that got breached years ago. Paper only.

**The signature you did not read.** Connecting your wallet to a random dApp and approving a transaction that grants unlimited spending permission on your USDT. If you do not understand what you are approving, do not approve it. Beginners have no reason to connect to dApps at all.

The pattern behind all four is social, not technical, and it is the one we broke down in [WhatsApp crypto scams in Cameroon](/blog/whatsapp-crypto-scams-cameroon/). Urgency, a helpful stranger, and one request that does not quite make sense. Our [safety guide](/safety/) is the checklist version.

**Update, 3 August 2026: what the Coldcard hack means for your wallet.** Since Friday 30 July, attackers have been draining bitcoin out of Coldcard hardware wallets. More than a thousand addresses have been hit and loss estimates run from 70 million to 114 million dollars, [reported by CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/08/03/bitcoin-ether-decline-as-coldcard-exploit-enters-a-fifth-day). The cause is narrow. A firmware bug from March 2021 made some Coldcard devices build their recovery phrase using predictable software randomness instead of true hardware randomness, so those words could be worked out later by an attacker. That is a fault in how one brand of device created the words. It is not evidence that holding your own keys is broken. Wallets whose recovery phrase was generated properly, including Trust Wallet on your phone, are not affected by this bug.

Two things follow for readers here, and neither is the one the headlines suggest. First, do not panic and move everything onto an exchange, which is what a lot of small holders did last week. You would be trading a bug that does not touch your wallet for the freeze risk that does. Second, expect the second wave, because it always comes. After every large hack the messages start on WhatsApp and Telegram: "security check", "migrate your wallet", "verify your seed to confirm you are safe". Every one of those is the support impostor described above, wearing a news headline. Nobody needs your twelve words, ever, for any reason.

## FAQ

### Is Trust Wallet legal in Cameroon?

Yes. Owning a wallet and holding crypto is not illegal in Cameroon. What the regional framework restricts is banks and licensed financial institutions dealing in virtual assets, which is why local banks stay out of it and why conversion happens through desks and peer to peer rather than through your bank branch. Nothing about installing a wallet on your own phone breaks a rule.

### What happens if I lose my phone?

Nothing, provided you have your twelve words on paper. You install Trust Wallet on a new phone, choose the import option, enter the words in the correct order, and your balance reappears exactly as it was. The wallet lives on the blockchain, not in the phone. If you lost the phone and never wrote the words down, the funds are unrecoverable, and no support team can help.

### Can I receive money from anyone with Trust Wallet?

Yes, from anyone in the world, as long as you both use the same network for the same coin. You give them your address for that specific network and the transfer settles in minutes without any bank in the middle. Just confirm the network in writing first and always test a new address with a small amount before a large one.

### Does Trust Wallet charge fees?

The app itself is free to install and free to receive into. What you pay is the network fee for sending, which goes to the blockchain and not to Trust Wallet, and that is why the network choice matters so much. Sending USDT on Tron costs roughly a dollar while the same transfer on Ethereum can cost several times more.

### Should a beginner in Cameroon use Trust Wallet or keep coins on an exchange?

For anything you plan to hold longer than a few days, the wallet is safer, because an exchange can freeze an account and a wallet cannot. Keep on a platform only what you are actively converting. New users often do the opposite, leaving everything on an exchange for convenience, and that is the balance that gets locked when a verification request arrives at the wrong moment.
