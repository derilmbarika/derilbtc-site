To pay school fees in Canada from Cameroon, you have four realistic routes: a bank wire from a Cameroonian bank, a money transfer operator like Western Union or Ria, the university's own payment portal such as Flywire, Convera or PayMyTuition, or a local desk that settles the invoice directly and sends you proof. For most families paying between 2,000,000 and 10,000,000 FCFA, the desk route and the university portal are the two that actually finish before the deadline. The bank wire is the one that gets rejected. The money transfer operator is the one that caps out.

The core problem is not the money. It is that Canadian universities want Canadian dollars, in a Canadian account, with a reference number matching your student ID, and almost nothing in Cameroonian retail banking is built to do that quickly. Every route converts XAF to CAD somewhere, and every route hides a different amount of that conversion in the rate rather than the fee line. Below: the four routes compared, a worked example on a 10,000 CAD invoice, what changed with IRCC financial checks on 24 July 2026, and the three failures our desk fixes every intake season.

Here is how the four routes compare on the things that decide whether your fees land on time. Costs are typical ranges our desk sees, not quotes, and every provider changes pricing.

Read the "funding it from Cameroon" row twice, because it is the trap nobody sees coming. Flywire, Convera and PayMyTuition are good systems and their markup is the lowest on the table. The catch is that they are payment rails, not funding rails: they expect money pushed in from a card or a bank account that can send internationally. If your Cameroonian card is domestic only, or your bank refuses the outbound transfer, the cheapest route on paper is one you cannot start.

## How much does it really cost to send 10,000 CAD in tuition?

Take a concrete first semester invoice of 10,000 CAD, which is normal for a Canadian college programme. In early August 2026 the mid market rate sits near 408 FCFA per Canadian dollar, so the raw value is about 4,080,000 FCFA. You can check the current level on the [Bank of Canada daily exchange rates](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/rates/exchange/) before you accept anyone's quote.

Nobody gives you the mid market rate. What you pay above it is the real cost, and it dwarfs the fee line.

**The bank wire.** A branch quotes a SWIFT fee that looks small, then applies a buying rate several percent off market, then the correspondent bank takes a cut in transit, then the Canadian institution may deduct a landing fee. On 4,080,000 FCFA, families routinely end up 250,000 to 480,000 FCFA worse off than mid market, and that is when it works. The bigger cost is the two weeks and the risk of the file coming back for another document.

**The money transfer operator.** Fast and familiar, but built for family support, not tuition. Limits mean a 10,000 CAD invoice becomes four or five transfers, each carrying its own spread, and the payout lands as cash or in a personal account rather than against your student number.

**The portal route.** The 1% to 3% markup is real and it is the best number here, if you can fund it.

**The desk route.** You send FCFA locally by MoMo, Orange Money or bank transfer, and the invoice is settled on the other side. Expect 2% to 5% all in, so roughly 80,000 to 200,000 FCFA on this size, quoted as one number in francs before you commit. One cost people forget: the MoMo leg. Withdrawing millions of francs in cash to hand over somewhere costs real money in withdrawal charges, plus the 0.2% state transfer levy that applies across the sector. Paying from your mobile money balance straight to a desk skips the withdrawal fee, worth tens of thousands of francs at tuition size.

## What changed with IRCC financial checks in July 2026?

This catches Cameroonian applicants right now, and it is new enough that most agents in Douala have not adjusted their advice yet.

On 24 July 2026, IRCC updated its operational guidance on assessing an applicant's financial resources. Three changes matter. Officers now examine the amount and the source of funds in all cases, not only in environments previously flagged as high risk. Applicants are asked for six months of bank statements including the submission month, up from four. And officers may request supplementary documents in any file rather than selectively.

The required amounts did not change. A sole applicant studying outside Quebec still has to show 22,895 CAD in living costs for the first year, a figure in force since 1 September 2025, on top of the first year of tuition and travel costs. Quebec sets its own and higher threshold, so confirm yours on the official [IRCC financial support page](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/study-permit/get-documents/financial-support.html) rather than on a forum.

The consequence is blunt. A large deposit appearing three days before submission with nothing behind it is now likelier to be questioned than it was in June. If a family is selling land, drawing on a business, or pooling contributions from relatives, assemble the paperwork explaining that alongside the money. This does not change how you pay the fees. It changes when you move them: earlier, and traceably.

## What goes wrong when paying Canadian tuition from Cameroon?

Three failures, and we see all three between June and September every year.

**The reference number is missing.** Money arrives in a university account with no student ID attached, so it sits unallocated while the student's status still shows unpaid. Every Canadian institution publishes a payment reference format. Take it from the invoice, not from a WhatsApp group, and confirm it appears on whatever your payer sends.

**The deadline was measured wrong.** Families count backwards from the fee date and forget that a bank wire's clock starts when the file is approved, not when it is submitted. Two weeks becomes three. If you are already inside two weeks, the bank route is off the table.

**The intermediary is not real.** Tuition season attracts impostors because the amounts are large, the families are anxious, and the sender is often a parent who has never moved money internationally. Anyone who cannot show a documented history, will not give an all in price in francs upfront, or pressures you because "the rate moves in one hour" is not who should be holding four million francs.

## FAQ

### Can I pay Canadian university tuition directly with bitcoin or USDT?

No. Canadian universities invoice and receive in Canadian dollars, and none of them take crypto at the registrar. What stablecoins do is move the value out of Cameroon quickly and cheaply so that a Canadian dollar payment can be made on the other side, either by a desk that settles the invoice for you or by a relative already in Canada. The school itself never touches crypto.

### How long before my fee deadline should I start paying from Cameroon?

Two weeks of margin if you are using a desk or a university payment portal, and a full month for a bank wire, because the wire clock only starts once your file is approved. During the June to September rush every route slows down. Starting early costs nothing and is the cheapest insurance in this process.

### How much money do I need to show for a Canadian study permit in 2026?

A sole applicant studying outside Quebec must show 22,895 Canadian dollars for living costs for the first year, on top of the first year of tuition and travel costs. That figure has applied since September 2025. Quebec sets a separate and higher threshold. Since 24 July 2026 officers also ask for six months of bank statements and examine the source of the funds in every case, so verify the current requirement on the official IRCC page before you file.

### Is it cheaper to send money to a relative in Canada and let them pay?

Often yes, if the relative is reliable and understands what they are agreeing to. You save the desk margin and the portal markup is low. What you take on instead is a compliance cost: a large inbound transfer converted and immediately sent to a university looks unusual to a Canadian bank, so your relative should keep the admission letter and invoice on hand. Many families use a desk for the first payment and switch to the relative route later.

### What happens if my bank rejects the international tuition transfer?

Nothing is lost except time. The funds normally return after several working days, minus whatever charges were already applied, and you restart with a different route. This is the most common reason a Cameroonian family misses a Canadian fee deadline, which is why a bank wire should never be your only plan inside the final two weeks.
