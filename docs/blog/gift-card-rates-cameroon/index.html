Gift Card Rates in Cameroon: What Your Card Is Really Worth

Gift card rates in Cameroon are never the face value of the card. A buyer quotes you a percentage of the dollar amount printed on the card, then pays you in FCFA at that day's dollar rate. In practice, a clean US physical card with its receipt lands between 60 and 75 percent of face value at our desk. An e-code with no proof of purchase, a UK card or a prepaid Visa lands far lower, sometimes under half. So a 100 dollar Amazon card is not 56,700 FCFA in your pocket. On a good day it is closer to 38,000 to 42,000 FCFA, and on a bad card it can be 25,000.

That gap shocks first-time sellers, so here is the honest reason for it. Nobody in Cameroon can spend a US gift card. The person buying it from you has to resell it into a market that can, and that market is mostly Nigeria. Everyone in that chain takes a cut, and the buyer carries the risk that the card was already redeemed, charged back or bought with a stolen credit card. The percentage you are quoted is the price of that risk plus everyone's margin. Below is what each brand pays, why the naira sets your offer, and the five things that quietly cut it before you even send the photo.

Two patterns are worth memorising because they hold for every brand.

Physical beats digital, always. A card you can photograph front and back, with the shop receipt, is worth more than the same value as an e-code in an email. The e-code is what a scammer sends. A physical card with a till receipt is much harder to fake, so it earns a better rate.

American beats British and European. A US card sells into the widest resale market. The same brand in a UK denomination is quoted lower every time, which surprises people whose relative sent the card from London in good faith.

Why is the Amazon gift card rate different from the iTunes rate?

Because the two cards get resold into completely different demand.

An Amazon card is close to cash for anyone who shops online, so resellers move it quickly. That liquidity pushes the Amazon gift card rate to the top of the ladder. An Apple card only buys inside Apple's ecosystem, and Apple is strict about redemption region and account history, so a buyer holds it longer and prices in that wait.

Then there is the rule almost nobody reads. Amazon's own gift card terms state that its cards cannot be resold or redeemed for cash, and Apple says the same. That does not make selling your own unwanted card a crime in Cameroon, but it explains why no formal, regulated marketplace exists for this in Central Africa. The trade runs on reputation between individuals, which is why the rate carries a risk premium and why who you sell to matters more than the number they quote.

To sell an iTunes card in Cameroon at a fair number, format matters more than brand. A physical Apple card with a receipt often beats an Amazon e-code, even though Amazon sits higher on the ladder.

Why do Cameroon gift card rates follow Nigeria?

Because the buyer of last resort is almost always Nigerian.

Cameroon has no domestic use for a US gift card and no local platform with enough volume to set its own price. So the chain runs like this: you sell to a Cameroonian desk, that desk resells into the Nigerian gift card market, and the Nigerian buyer is pricing in naira per dollar. Every step of that chain is a spread, and the number at the end reaches you converted into FCFA.

The practical consequence is one most sellers never connect. When the naira weakens sharply, Nigerian rates in naira per dollar go up, which looks like good news, but the dollar value the Nigerian buyer will actually pay often falls at the same time. Your FCFA offer drops for a reason that has nothing to do with Cameroon or your card. If you have ever been told "the rate is bad this week" with no explanation, this is usually it. The second lever is the dollar itself. Your card is a dollar asset and you get paid in francs, so the USD to XAF rate is baked into your offer. As of 6 August 2026 the dollar sits near 568 FCFA, which you can check on a neutral source like Wise's USD to XAF page before anyone quotes you. It takes ten seconds and it stops a buyer from quietly using a stale 540 in their arithmetic. What lowers your gift card rate?

Five things, and four of them are in your control.

  1. No receipt. This is the big one. A card without proof of purchase is treated as possibly stolen, and the discount for that suspicion is heavy. Photograph the receipt at the till, before you leave the shop, before anything else happens to it.
  2. A scratched or damaged code. If the silver panel is over-scratched and one character is ambiguous, verification takes longer and the offer drops. Scratch with a coin, gently, once.
  3. Small denominations. Ten 10 dollar cards are worth less than one 100 dollar card, because each code is checked separately and carries its own risk. Ask relatives abroad to send fewer, larger cards.
  4. Age. A card sitting in a drawer for eight months is a worse card. Balances get drained, promotions expire, and the buyer cannot know what happened in that time. Sell within weeks, not seasons.
  5. Weekend timing. Buyers hold wider spreads when the resale market is closed and they cannot offload quickly. Monday to Thursday quotes beat Sunday quotes.

One thing that does not lower your rate: asking for the number in writing before you send the code. Any real buyer expects that.

How do you get paid, and how do you avoid getting robbed?

Payment is MTN MoMo or Orange Money, occasionally a local bank transfer for larger amounts. That part is simple. The dangerous part is sequencing, because a gift card is the one asset that is fully spent the instant someone reads the code off a photograph.

This is the structural trap. When you sell bitcoin, the buyer cannot take it without you signing a transaction, as we explain in how to sell bitcoin for MoMo. When you sell a gift card, the buyer can drain it the moment they see the image, then tell you the card was empty on arrival. There is no blockchain to check and no way to prove otherwise. That asymmetry is behind most gift card losses we hear about, and it is a close cousin of the tricks in WhatsApp crypto scams in Cameroon.

So the rules our desk gives every seller are these. Agree the exact FCFA figure in writing first, not a percentage and not "we will see after checking". Never send a full code to someone you have not traded with before. Start with one small card to build a record. Confirm the MoMo credit in your own balance, not in a screenshot, because forged transfer screenshots are the standard tool of this trade and are covered on our MoMo scams page. A buyer who insists on the code first every time is putting all the risk on you.

There is a bigger strategic move here too. Many people selling cards are trying to hold value, not spend francs, and a gift card is a terrible store of value: it expires, it gets frozen, and it loses 30 percent the moment you convert. If your goal is to keep dollars, converting a card into USDT you actually control beats sitting on plastic, and the choice between stablecoins and bitcoin is covered in bitcoin vs USDT in Cameroon. Whatever you decide, run the counterparty through the checks on our safety page first.

FAQ

Which gift card has the best rate in Cameroon?
Amazon US physical cards with a receipt generally sit at the top, followed by Razer Gold PINs and Steam. Apple and iTunes are mid-table, Google Play sits below them because it is region locked, and prepaid Visa or Mastercard cards are weakest because they are slow to liquidate. Brand matters less than format and region, though: a US physical card with a receipt beats a UK e-code of the same brand nearly every time.
How much is a 100 dollar gift card worth in FCFA?
At a dollar rate near 568 FCFA, 100 dollars of face value is about 56,800 FCFA, but you never receive face value. At a typical 60 to 75 percent band for a clean US physical card, expect roughly 34,000 to 42,500 FCFA. E-codes without receipts, and UK or European cards, fall well below that. Ask for the FCFA figure rather than the percentage, because the percentage hides which dollar rate the buyer used.
Is selling gift cards legal in Cameroon?
There is no Cameroonian law that criminalises selling a gift card you legitimately own. What exists is a contractual restriction from the issuers, since Amazon and Apple both prohibit resale in their terms, which is why no regulated marketplace operates here. The real legal risk is different from what people assume: it comes from handling cards bought with stolen credit cards, which is fraud. Only sell cards you or your family actually purchased, and keep the receipt.
Why did the buyer say my gift card was already used?
Sometimes it is true, especially with cards sent from abroad months earlier or bought with a payment method later reversed. Sometimes it is a lie told after the buyer redeemed the code themselves. You cannot tell the two apart after the fact, which is why sequencing matters. Check the balance yourself on the issuer's official site first, and never sell a code to a stranger with no trading history.
Can I get paid for a gift card in MTN MoMo or Orange Money?
Yes. Mobile money is the standard payout in Cameroon, with local bank transfer used for larger amounts. Confirm the money in your own MoMo balance before you release the code, never trusting a screenshot. Remember too that the state levy and operator charges apply, so what lands in your wallet is slightly below the agreed number.
