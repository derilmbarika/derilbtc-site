Yes. As of July 2026, bitcoin is legal to own, buy, sell and hold in Cameroon. No law in Cameroon or in the wider CEMAC zone makes it a crime for a private individual to keep bitcoin in a wallet or to trade it with another person. What does not exist is the opposite of a ban: bitcoin is not legal tender, no shop is obliged to accept it, and no regulator will help you if a trade goes wrong. The question "is bitcoin legal in Cameroon" really has two halves, and most confusing articles online only answer one of them.

Here is the short version our desk gives on WhatsApp ten times a week. Holding and trading crypto as a person: allowed, unregulated, entirely at your own risk. Running a business that holds other people's crypto, exchanges it, or advises on it: that falls inside CEMAC financial-market rules and needs authorisation. Your bank: still fenced off from crypto by BEAC's position, which is why your Cameroonian debit card keeps failing on exchange deposit pages. Everything below explains each half, with what actually happens in practice.

## Is bitcoin legal in Cameroon for ordinary people?

Nothing in Cameroonian law criminalises owning bitcoin. There is no registration to complete, no permit to obtain, and no threshold above which private ownership becomes illegal. Thousands of Cameroonians hold BTC and USDT today, and our desk has never once seen a client face a legal problem simply for owning crypto.

But "not illegal" is not the same as "protected". Crypto is not recognised as legal tender in the CEMAC zone, which has six member states sharing the FCFA and the [Bank of Central African States](https://www.beac.int/) as their central bank. That means:

| Question | Position in Cameroon, July 2026 |
|---|---|
| Can you own bitcoin? | Yes, no law forbids it |
| Can you trade it person to person? | Yes, it is not a criminal act |
| Is it legal tender? | No, nobody must accept it as payment |
| Can a licensed bank handle it for you? | No, BEAC restricts supervised institutions |
| Is there a consumer-protection regulator for crypto? | No, you carry the full risk |
| Do businesses offering crypto services need authorisation? | Yes, under CEMAC financial-market rules |

Read that last row twice. It is the row most people get wrong, and it is the row that decides who you should trade with.

## What does BEAC actually say about crypto?

BEAC has been consistent and cautious for years. Fintech companies across the region have repeatedly pushed the central bank to write proper crypto rules, and BEAC has repeatedly declined to open that door. Its concern is capital flight from the FCFA zone and money laundering exposure, not the technology itself.

The practical consequence of that stance shows up in your daily life more than in any legal text. Banks supervised by BEAC and COBAC are restricted from facilitating virtual-asset transactions. That is why:

- Your Cameroonian Visa or Mastercard is declined on most international exchange deposit pages.
- SWIFT wires flagged as crypto-related get returned or questioned.
- Some banks quietly close accounts that show heavy, unexplained crypto-pattern flows.

None of that is a law telling you that bitcoin is banned. It is a supervisory instruction telling banks to stay out. The difference matters, because it means the rails you can actually use are MoMo, Orange Money and cash, not your bank card. That is precisely why the P2P and OTC market exists here in the shape it does. ## What changed for crypto in 2026?

Two things moved this year, and neither is a ban.

First, the 2026 Finance Law brought digital platforms into the tax net properly for the first time. Non-resident online platforms that reach a significant economic presence in Cameroon, measured by a consumer base of at least 1,000 users or annual pre-tax revenue of at least 50 million FCFA, now face a minimum corporate tax on Cameroon-sourced revenue. [Ecofin Agency reported the 3% minimum rate](https://www.ecofinagency.com/news-finances/0201-51740-cameroon-introduces-3-minimum-tax-on-non-resident-digital-platforms) when the law came into force. That is aimed at foreign platforms, not at you personally, but it signals the direction: the state wants visibility over digital money flows.

Second, CEMAC's financial-market framework has tightened around anyone providing virtual-asset services. Custody, exchange, transfer and advisory activity all sit inside the regulated perimeter now. A "trader" running an unregistered pool of clients' funds is exposed in a way a private holder is not.

## Do you pay tax on bitcoin profits in Cameroon?

There is no dedicated crypto capital-gains regime in Cameroon as of July 2026. What exists is general income-tax law, and the tax administration's position is that realised gains from commercial activity are taxable income like any other. If you buy bitcoin, hold it and sell it once at a profit, nobody is currently coming for you. If you trade professionally, take client funds, or run crypto as a business, you are conducting taxable commercial activity and should speak to a Cameroonian accountant, not to a WhatsApp group.

## FAQ

### Can I go to prison for owning bitcoin in Cameroon?

No. As of July 2026 no Cameroonian law criminalises owning, buying or selling bitcoin as a private individual. Crypto simply sits outside the regulated financial system, which means it is neither banned nor protected.

### Why does my Cameroonian bank card fail on crypto exchanges?

Because BEAC restricts supervised financial institutions from facilitating virtual-asset transactions. Your card is not broken and you are not blacklisted. The bank is following its central-bank supervisory position, which is why most Cameroonians fund trades with MTN MoMo, Orange Money or cash instead.

### Is bitcoin recognised as money in the CEMAC zone?

No. Bitcoin is not legal tender anywhere in CEMAC, so no merchant, employer or landlord is obliged to accept it. You can agree to use it privately with someone who is willing, but there is no legal obligation on either side.

### Will CEMAC regulate crypto soon?

Fintech companies in the region have pushed for clear rules and BEAC has so far declined to create them. Nothing suggests an imminent framework. Assume the current situation, legal to hold and unregulated in practice, continues for now, and check for updates before making a large commitment.

### Do I have to declare my crypto in Cameroon?

There is no crypto-specific declaration form today. General income-tax rules still apply to gains from commercial activity, so anyone trading as a business should speak to a Cameroonian accountant. Keeping your own record of dates, FCFA amounts and counterparties is sensible regardless of your size.
