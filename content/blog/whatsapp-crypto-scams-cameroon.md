## What do crypto scams on WhatsApp look like in Cameroon?

They are not sophisticated. They are fast, polite and repetitive, and that is why they keep working.

**The doubling scheme.** Somebody adds you to a group, or a friend's hacked account messages you directly. Send 50,000 FCFA, receive 150,000 FCFA in 48 hours. There are screenshots of happy payouts and a countdown to a "closing" window. Some victims are even paid the first time, on purpose, so they come back with a bigger amount and bring friends.

**The better rate than everybody.** A "trader" offers you a rate nobody else in Douala or Yaounde can offer, plus instant delivery, plus no verification. A real desk makes its living on a small spread. Anybody who can beat the whole market has a different business model, and you are it.

**The cloned desk.** This one hurts. Someone copies a real desk's logo, name and profile photo, changes one digit in the phone number, and answers exactly like the real thing. They usually reach out to you first, often right after you posted a crypto question in a public group. That is why we say plainly on our [about page](/about/) which number and which people are actually us. Check the number against the site, not against the profile picture.

**The stuck withdrawal.** You are shown a dashboard with a growing balance on some platform you have never heard of. When you try to withdraw, there is a tax, an activation fee, a network fee, an upgrade. Each payment unlocks a new one. The balance was never real. It was a web page.

**The fake platform link.** A message announces a new savings or loan product, often borrowing MTN or Orange branding, and the link asks for your Mobile Money PIN. MTN Cameroon states the rule directly on its own [customer information page](https://mtn.cm/useful-information/): mobile money providers and banks will never ask a client for their PIN. Anyone who asks is a thief, with no exceptions.

**The slow friend.** Weeks of ordinary conversation, sometimes romance, sometimes a business idea, before crypto is ever mentioned. By the time the ask arrives you are not dealing with a stranger anymore, which is exactly the point of the weeks.

## How do you recognise a fake crypto trader?

Look at behaviour, not at branding. Logos, gold badges, "certified" banners and Telegram testimonial walls take ten minutes to produce. Behaviour is much harder to fake.

A fake crypto trader messages you first. A real desk gets contacted by clients and does not need to hunt for strangers in groups.

A fake trader will not do a plain voice call, and definitely not a video call, on the number you were given. There is always a reason: bad network, in a meeting, phone charging, currently at the bank.

A fake trader pushes on time. Rate expires in ten minutes. Slot closing. Last two spots. Real rates do move, so a desk may say a quote holds for a short window, but a real desk will happily requote you tomorrow instead of pressuring you today.

A fake trader gets defensive when you ask basic questions. Where is your office. What is your full legal name. Can I pay 10,000 FCFA first as a test. Those questions cost an honest business nothing and cost a scammer their afternoon.

A fake trader asks for payment to a MoMo name that does not match the person you are speaking to, then explains it away. "That is my brother's account" is the most common sentence in this market, and it is a refusal, not an explanation. We wrote about the same pattern from the seller's side in our guide to the [risks of Binance P2P in Cameroon](/blog/binance-p2p-cameroon-risks/).

And the biggest one: a fake trader promises a return. Nobody who actually trades crypto guarantees profit, because nobody controls the price. Guaranteed returns are the single clearest signal in this entire article.

## Why is Cameroon such an easy target for these scams?

Three local realities make WhatsApp fraud unusually profitable here, and it helps to name them.

First, mobile money is final. Once an MTN MoMo or Orange Money transfer lands, only the recipient can send it back. There is no chargeback, no card issuer, no reversal window. That is also why our [MoMo scams page](/momo-scams-cameroon/) spends so much time on verifying before you pay rather than recovering after.

Second, there is no regulator standing behind your crypto trade. Banks in the CEMAC zone are kept away from virtual assets by the regional supervisor, in line with the position of the [Bank of Central African States](https://www.beac.int/), so there is no licensed local exchange and no authority that will reimburse you. Trading as an individual is not illegal, but you are your own compliance department.

Third, WhatsApp is where business genuinely happens in Cameroon. Suppliers, landlords, transport, school groups and real crypto desks all operate there, so a scam message does not feel out of place. It looks like every other message in your day.

None of that means WhatsApp is unsafe to trade on. Our own desk runs on it. It means the platform provides zero protection, so the protection has to come from your process. ## What should you do if you already sent the money?

Move in the first hour. Recovery odds fall fast, but they are not zero if the funds are still sitting in a mobile money wallet.

1. Call your operator immediately and report the transaction as fraud. For MTN, the support line is 8787. Give the transaction ID, the receiving number and the time. Ask them to flag the wallet.
2. Report it as cybercrime. MTN's own guidance points customers to the national response team on 8202 or alert@cirt.antic.cm, available at any hour.
3. Screenshot everything before you are blocked: the chat, the profile, the phone number, the transaction confirmations. Do not delete the conversation out of anger.
4. File a report at the nearest police or gendarmerie post. It matters for the operator's investigation and it matters if the same number is hitting other people.
5. Warn your circle, plainly. These numbers work through groups, and one honest message stops several more losses.
6. Refuse every "recovery agent" who appears afterwards. They watch scam reports and target people twice. There is no service that reverses blockchain transactions, ever.

Then rebuild the habit that failed. Most people who lose money on WhatsApp did not lack intelligence. They skipped one check because the person seemed nice and the moment felt urgent. ## FAQ

### Can I get my money back after a WhatsApp crypto scam in Cameroon?

Sometimes, and only if you move very quickly. If the FCFA is still sitting in the scammer's mobile money wallet and you report it to the operator within minutes, the wallet can be frozen while the case is reviewed. Once the money has been cashed out at an agent or converted to crypto, it is effectively gone. Anything already sent on a blockchain cannot be reversed by anyone, including the exchange, the network or the police.

### How can I tell if a crypto trader on WhatsApp is real?

Check identity, voice and history before money. A real trader gives you a full legal name and a physical location you can verify away from the chat, answers a live phone call on the number you were given, has a number with months or years of use behind it, and receives payment on a MoMo account in the same name. A real trader is also completely relaxed about a small test trade and about you deciding tomorrow.

### Is it safe to buy bitcoin over WhatsApp at all?

Yes, if the counterparty is a known desk with a real identity, and no, if it is a stranger who messaged you first. The platform itself protects nobody. It is a chat app, not an escrow service. Everything depends on who is on the other end and whether you verified them before the first franc moved. Treat the first trade with any new counterparty as the cost of finding out.

### Why do scammers ask for a fee before releasing my crypto profit?

Because the profit never existed. The dashboard showing your balance is a web page they control, and the numbers on it mean nothing. The fee request is the actual business: tax, activation, network charge, VIP upgrade, each one designed to pull one more payment from somebody who does not want to accept that the first payment is already lost. Once fees start, stop paying and report the number.
